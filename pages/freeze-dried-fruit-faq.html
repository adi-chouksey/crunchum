Freeze-dried fruit FAQs

What is freeze-dried fruit?

Freeze-dried fruit is fresh fruit that has been frozen and then dried under a vacuum, so the ice turns straight into vapour and almost all the water is removed without heat. The fruit keeps its shape, colour and flavour and becomes light and crunchy. Crunchum freeze-dried fruit is 100% fruit: no added sugar, no preservatives, no oil.

What is the difference between freeze-dried fruit and dried fruit?

Freeze-dried fruit is crunchy and light, while regular dried fruit is chewy and dense. Regular (dehydrated) fruit is dried with warm air, which darkens it and changes the taste, and it often has added sugar, syrup, oil or sulphites. Freeze-drying uses cold and a vacuum instead, so the fruit stays crisp and close to fresh in colour and flavour. Crunchum dried mango, for example, snaps like a chip instead of chewing like toffee.

Does freeze-dried fruit contain sugar?

Freeze-dried fruit contains only the natural sugars of the fruit; Crunchum adds no sugar, syrup or sweeteners. Because the water is removed, sugar per 100 g looks higher than in fresh fruit: from about 34 g in Crunchum strawberry slices to about 77 g in mango. A typical 20 g pouch contains roughly 7 to 15 g of naturally occurring sugar.

How many calories are in freeze-dried fruit?

Crunchum freeze-dried fruit has about 270 to 375 kcal per 100 g, or roughly 54 to 75 kcal per 20 g pouch. Strawberry slices are the lowest (about 270 kcal per 100 g) and mango is the highest (about 375 kcal per 100 g). Values per 100 g are higher than fresh fruit only because the water has been removed.

Is freeze-dried fruit as nutritious as fresh fruit?

Freeze-drying removes water but keeps the fruit's fibre and natural sugars, so freeze-dried fruit is very close to fresh fruit in composition, just much more concentrated. Because it is so light, compare portions rather than 100 g values: a 20 g pouch of Crunchum fruit is made from a much larger amount of fresh fruit. There is nothing added, so the ingredient list is simply the fruit.

Which freeze-dried fruits are high in fibre?

In the Crunchum range, chikoo (sapodilla) is the highest in fibre at about 36.7 g per 100 g, followed by strawberry slices at about 26.8 g and mango at about 6.3 g per 100 g. All three meet the EU 'high fibre' level of at least 6 g per 100 g.

Is Crunchum vegan and gluten-free?

Yes. Every Crunchum product is a single fruit with nothing added, so it is suitable for vegans and contains no gluten ingredients. There are also no added sugars, preservatives, artificial colours or flavours.

How long does freeze-dried fruit last?

Unopened, Crunchum freeze-dried fruit keeps for up to 12 months in a cool, dry place. Once opened, reseal the pouch tightly and eat it within a few days: freeze-dried fruit draws moisture from the air and slowly loses its crunch.

How do you eat freeze-dried fruit?

Eat it straight from the pouch as a crunchy snack, or add it to yoghurt, kwark, overnight oats, muesli, granola, smoothie bowls, desserts or baking. You can crush it into a powder to colour and flavour icing or drinks, or add a little water to soften it again.

What are chikoo and jamun?

Chikoo (also called sapota or sapodilla) is a tropical fruit with a soft, caramel-like, brown-sugar sweetness. Jamun (Java plum) is a dark purple Indian fruit with a bold tangy-sweet taste and a harvest that lasts only a few weeks a year. Crunchum freeze-dries both into crunchy cubes so they can be enjoyed all year, including in Europe, where they are hard to find fresh.
